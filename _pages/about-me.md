I'm **Dr Raahat Manrai, PhD** — an online counsellor and the founder of **North Star Counselling**, based in Edinburgh. I offer confidential, compassionate support to clients worldwide through secure video sessions, and I bring the same care and attention to my work as a mental health researcher at the University of Edinburgh.

Alongside my counselling practice, I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Division of Psychiatry, where I specialise in qualitative and participatory research methods, particularly co-production with people who have lived experience of mental health difficulties. My research spans developmental trauma in care-experienced young people to sleep and circadian health in bipolar disorder. That depth of academic training informs how I practise: thoughtful, evidence-aware, and always centred on the person in front of me.

## Education and qualifications

I completed my **PhD in Clinical Psychology** at the University of Edinburgh (2020–2023), with a thesis on honouring the voices of care-experienced young people with a history of developmental trauma. My doctoral work used participatory action research and co-production with a third-sector organisation, and led to a Best Practice Framework developed with adult stakeholders.

Before that, I earned an **MSc (Hons) in Children and Young People's Mental Health and Psychological Practice** from Edinburgh (Merit), and a **BA (Hons) in Psychology** from Panjab University, India (First Class Honours, accredited by the British Psychological Society).

I am an **Associate Fellow of the Higher Education Academy** (Advance HE), and have been recognised for my teaching and community work, including a nomination for Best Student Tutor (EUSA Teaching Awards) and awards for ConveRACEions and Research Bow.

## Research and academic work

My current research includes **AMBIENT-BD**, a Wellcome Trust funded study on sleep and circadian rhythms in bipolar disorder, where I collect longitudinal data using wearables and nearables, facilitate co-production with people with bipolar disorder, and lead Lived Experience Advisory Panel meetings. I am also Principal Investigator on **SubSleep**, an Edinburgh Mental Health Network seed-funded project examining subjective experiences of sleep in people with bipolar disorder through semi-structured interviews, photovoice, and participatory methods.

My PhD research conceptualised developmental trauma exposure in care-experienced young people and created a Best Practice Framework through co-production with stakeholders. I have received seed funding and bursaries as lead or co-applicant on several projects, including work on racial equality in research through **ConveRACEions**, which I co-founded.

Beyond my own projects, I serve as ECR Representative on the Edinburgh Mental Health Network Steering Committee and as research coordinator for *On the Level*, a digital magazine by Bipolar Scotland. For a full list of publications, grants, and conference presentations, see my [CV](/cv/).

## Teaching and direct practice

I have supervised MSc students and tutored postgraduate courses at the University of Edinburgh, including Evidence Based Psychological Interventions, Research Methods in Applied Psychology, Typical and Atypical Child Development, and Trauma and Resilience in Developmental Context. I have also supervised work on trauma-informed, anti-racist practice and early adversity and sleep.

Alongside academia, I have worked directly with young people for many years. Since 2021 I have been a **youth worker** at Edinburgh City Youth Cafe (6VT), supporting care-experienced young people through drop-in nights, mental health workshops, and practical guidance on housing, finances, and relationships. Earlier, I worked as an after-school playworker with children aged 5–12, including those with Down Syndrome, autism, and conduct disorders. This frontline experience grounds my counselling in relational, trauma-aware practice.

I work in **English** (fluent), **Hindi** (native), and **French** (intermediate).
